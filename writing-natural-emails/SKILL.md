# Writing Natural Emails

## Principle

Write the shortest complete email that fits the recipient, relationship, purpose, and stakes. Sound like one person writing to another, not a template performing warmth.

## Process

1. Identify the recipient, relationship, purpose, and any real ask.
2. When referring to the recipient's work, inspect it and choose one relevant detail. Never invent familiarity.
3. Lead with the actual reason for writing, not a ceremonial opener.
4. Connect evidence to purpose: explain why the detail made this email worth sending.
5. Make the ask concrete. If there is no ask, let the email end once the point lands.
6. Remove generic praise, repeated context, sales language, and phrases that could fit any recipient.
7. Write the short, specific subject after the body.

Return the sendable subject and email first as plain, copy-ready text. Do not wrap it in a Markdown blockquote (`>`), bullet list, or decorative container unless the user asks for that format. Mention missing facts separately only when they block an honest draft.

## Voice

- Match the relationship. Warm professional is not the same as Slack; formal does not mean stiff.
- Use contractions and conversational connectors when they fit Neil's voice.
- Do not force typos, filler, self-interruptions, or quirks as proof of being human.
- Keep ordinary emails in paragraphs. Use headings or lists only when the content truly needs scanning.
- Use a brief, relationship-appropriate close; omit it in an ongoing thread when natural.
- Prefer one exact observation over several vague compliments.

## Common mistakes

| Avoid | Do instead |
|---|---|
| “I've been following your work” after seeing one repo | Say where you found it and mention the exact detail you noticed |
| “Would love to compare notes” | Ask a real question, propose a next step, or omit the empty ask |
| “No pitch” | Make the email itself clearly non-salesy |
| A polished summary of every feature | Keep only what explains why the recipient should care |
| “Best regards” by default | Choose a close that fits the actual relationship |
| A subject with a tagline or forced colon | Use a short phrase naming the real topic |

## Example

Subject: Supabase on CNPG

Found your project while looking into Supabase on CNPG. The giant ConfigMap full of role SQL made me laugh—I started in almost exactly the same place before letting CNPG own the roles.

I've been building something adjacent: [link]. Thought I'd send it over since we seem to have hit some of the same sharp edges.

Cheers,  
Neil
